
Build a polished, portfolio-quality finance SaaS dashboard concept for a product designer who specializes in dashboards, customer portals, financial products, and other data-heavy interfaces. This should feel like a real fintech or finance-operations product, not a marketing site.
The main goal is to create one strong dashboard concept I can use in my portfolio and as proof of capability in Upwork bids. The design should instantly communicate: “this designer thinks like a product designer, not a generalist web designer.”
Product direction
Create a fictional finance product with a modern, trustworthy brand name such as:
•	LedgerView.
•	FinFlow.
•	CapitalOps.
•	TreasuryBoard.
•	SpendPilot.
Choose one and make it feel like a real B2B finance platform used by finance teams, operators, or executives.
Core experience
Design a dashboard with these screens:
•	Overview dashboard.
•	Cash flow or spend analytics page.
•	Transactions or invoice table.
•	Account / vendor / customer detail page.
•	Multi-step approval or onboarding workflow.
•	Notifications / audit trail panel.
•	Settings page.
Finance dashboard content
Use realistic fintech and finance-ops widgets such as:
•	Cash balance cards.
•	Spend vs budget charts.
•	Revenue or expense trends.
•	Pending approvals.
•	Recent transactions.
•	Invoice status table.
•	Risk or compliance flags.
•	Audit logs.
•	Breakdown by department, vendor, or category.
•	Saved filters and search.
Multi-step workflow
Add a user-friendly multi-step flow inside the dashboard for a finance task such as:
•	onboarding a vendor,
•	approving an invoice,
•	reviewing a payment request,
•	setting up a customer account,
•	configuring spending controls,
•	or completing a treasury setup.
The flow should have 4–6 steps with:
•	A clear progress indicator.
•	Back and Next buttons.
•	Step-by-step validation.
•	Inline error messages.
•	Preserved form state when going backward.
•	A final review step.
•	A success confirmation state.
Validation should happen on the current step before allowing the user to continue. Do not wait until the end to show errors. Make the first invalid field obvious, keep error messages concise, and ensure the user never loses valid input when correcting mistakes.
UX requirements
Make the form feel calm, reliable, and low-friction. Use friendly microcopy, clear labels, accessible controls, and responsive behavior. Support keyboard navigation, and make the process feel guided rather than restrictive.
Product design quality
The dashboard should look dense, intelligent, and operational, with strong hierarchy and a clean visual system. Favor a neutral palette with one confident accent color, and use finance-appropriate styling that signals trust, precision, and control. Avoid decorative landing-page patterns. This should look like a serious SaaS product built for real financial work.
